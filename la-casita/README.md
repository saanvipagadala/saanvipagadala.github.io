## 🎯 The Question

Google and Apple build offices that are famous for being green.

**A third grader asked whether her own house was anything like them.**

Not by reading about it. By walking out to the electric meter, writing down the
reading, running one appliance for exactly one hour, and writing the reading
down again. Sixteen times, over three months.

## 🔌 The Experiment

| | |
|---|---|
| ⚡ **What she metered** | 8 big appliances: dryer, AC, oven, LED lights, water heater, Tesla charger, fridge, dishwasher |
| 📅 **When** | December 2024 to February 2025, each appliance twice on different dates |
| 🧪 **Controls** | One hour each, everything else switched off, same house, same way every time |
| 🧮 **The maths** | kWh per hour → kWh per month → dollars → carbon |

## 📊 What She Found

| Finding | The number |
|---|---|
| 🚗 **Most expensive thing in the house** | Tesla charger, **$53 a month** |
| 👕 **Most expensive hour** | Laundry dryer, **$2.27 an hour of use** |
| 💡 **Cheapest to run** | LED lights, **$8 a month** |
| 🌍 **Carbon is not a rounding error** | **$95** of carbon against a **$143** electricity bill |

Then the part that makes it a science project rather than a list: for every
appliance, a sustainable alternative, and an honest verdict on whether it would
actually work in her house. Sun-drying clothes, yes. Solar cooker instead of the
oven, no, not always. Solar water heater, no, too hard to install.

## ✨ Part II: La Casita

The experiment found the problems. Part two builds the answer: a Disney-inspired
smart home, in a dollhouse, that she wired and coded herself.

| | |
|---|---|
| 🧚 **Mirabel** | A Disney-themed AI home assistant, named after *Encanto*, who takes voice commands and runs the house |
| 🔌 **Raspberry Pi Pico W** | Controls every light in the home |
| 📱 **iPhone app** | Sends the voice commands |
| 👀 **Face recognition** | Opens doors and starts conversations |

And a list of what goes in next: hydroponics, a clay fridge that needs no
electricity, an atmospheric water generator, composting, solar, rainwater
harvesting, and a house designed to need no air conditioning at all.

## 🚀 Afterwards: the Gemma 3n Impact Challenge

The science fair was not the end of it. La Casita was adapted and entered into
Kaggle and Google's
[**Gemma 3n Impact Challenge**](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/google-gemma-3n-hackathon),
a competition for projects built on Google's on-device AI model, alongside
[**600+ entries**](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/developers-changing-lives-with-gemma-3n/)
from developers around the world. Mirabel was rebuilt on Gemma 3n, so the house thinks for
itself without sending anything over the internet.
