5 Rules for Using Technology in Class

February 2026 · 4th grade

Winner, ORISE Technology in the Classroom Student Challenge

ORISE, the Oak Ridge Institute for Science and Education, runs national challenges for K–12 students through the year. Each one asks for a piece of real work rather than a quiz: a poster, a design, a piece of writing about the future.

The Technology in the Classroom Student Challenge, in February 2026, asked a question every classroom is arguing about: what are the rules for using technology at school? Students in grades K–5 were asked to make a poster of those rules, and to explain the thinking behind each one.

Eight winners were chosen nationally, two per grade band. This poster took one of the K–5 prizes.

5 Rules for Using Technology in Class

Together we can use tech the right way!
Created by Saanvi  |  4th Grade  |  California
RULE 1
Use Technology for Learning
Classroom devices like tablets and laptops are tools for schoolwork, research, and learning, not for playing games or scrolling social media. When everyone stays focused on learning, the whole class moves forward together and gets more done during school time.
RULE 2
Handle Devices with Care
Always use technology with clean hands, carry devices with two hands, and never eat or drink near them. School devices are shared by many students, so taking good care of them means everyone can keep using them and the school does not have to spend money on repairs.
RULE 3
Ask Before You Click
Always check with your teacher before visiting an unfamiliar website, downloading a file, or clicking on a pop-up. Not every website is safe or trustworthy, so asking first keeps your personal information protected and helps you avoid scams or harmful content online.
RULE 4
Be Kind Online
Treat every person with kindness and respect in messages, comments, and shared documents, just like you would face-to-face. Words on a screen can still hurt real feelings. Being a good digital citizen means thinking before you type and making the internet a friendlier place.
RULE 5
Take Screen Breaks
Every 20 minutes, look away from your screen for at least 20 seconds and focus on something far away. Too much screen time without breaks can cause eye strain, headaches, and tiredness. Protecting your health helps you stay alert, focused, and ready to learn all day long.
