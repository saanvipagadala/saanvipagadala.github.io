What we found out

Each question below was answered by the same rule, written down before anyone looked at the numbers, so a disappointing answer counts exactly as much as a good one.

Did the regolith turn into something that grows?

Yes

Every treated cup grew more than every bare-regolith cup, 11.1 times heavier on average, with no overlap at all. Which part did it is the next question.

Did the worms add anything the food did not?

Yes

Every worm cup beat every cup that got the same food without worms. The worms did something the food alone did not.

Were the castings enough on their own?

No

Castings alone fell behind every worm cup. The living, burrowing worm adds something the castings do not.

How sure can we be, with three cups? Every comparison above asks one thing: did the two groups overlap? If the worst cup in one group still beat the best cup in the other, that is as strong as three cups can ever get. If the ranges cross, the honest answer is that we could not tell them apart and not that they are the same.

A difference also has to be big enough to matter. A result here needs a gap of at least 10% of the whole scale, bare regolith to real potting soil, which is 0.634 g, so at least 0.063 g. Without that rule, plants could triple in weight, still be dead, and get called a success.
