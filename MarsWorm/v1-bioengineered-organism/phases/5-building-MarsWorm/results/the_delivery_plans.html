Five ways to build it, and the lab picks one

Stage 5 orders six cassettes of DNA. Getting each one inside a living microbe is a separate problem, and there is more than one way to do it. All five are worked out here, end to end.

The question The short answer
Why is there more than one plan? The lab decides how DNA gets into the cell, not us
What decides whether a plan works? One number: the biggest piece of DNA it can deliver at once
Do the rounds change between plans? No. Only the delivery changes
Which would we choose? A long-synthesis vendor, with overlapping fragments as the fallback
What would we tell the lab? Order round 1 first, whichever plan they pick

Why there is more than one plan

The lab chooses how DNA gets into the cell, not us. That depends on their equipment, what they have done before, and what they are willing to try, none of which is knowable from here.

So every route worth considering is worked out end to end below, ranked by our preference with the reasons written down. Whichever the lab picks, the rounds, the tests and the biology are the same. Only the delivery changes.

The one number that decides a plan

How big a piece of DNA the route can deliver in one go. Every round has to fit under it, and a round is a whole cassette, not just the genes:

What every cassette carries Size
Two homology arms 1,000 bp
Antibiotic marker 1,000 bp
Reporter, one colour per round 720 bp
Promoter, shared by all the genes in the round 150 bp
One RBS per gene 25 bp each
Terminator 50 bp
≈3,020 bp of overhead on a 4-gene round

That overhead is charged per round, not per gene. Which is why collapsing rounds only helps when the ceiling is high enough to hold the bigger cassette. Under a low ceiling, collapsing costs rounds instead of saving them.

The rounds are the same in every plan

Rounds are grouped by what the genes do, so each round has one coherent test. A round that fails should point at one idea, not five unrelated ones.

The round The genes Why these belong together The test
1 Water: freezing and drying 4: cspB, Antifreeze protein, CAS31, TPS1
3,228 bp of genes		 Both are the same problem: what happens to a cell when its water freezes, or when its water leaves. Stage 4 already has a protocol for each. Freeze it and thaw it; dry it out. Modified strain against plain, side by side. Stage 4’s two protocols, unchanged.
2 Damage: radiation, chemicals and repair 6: irrE, dps, dnaK, ibpA, SODCC, katA
5,538 bp of genes		 Every gene here does one job: protect molecules under attack, or mend them afterwards. Splitting them would test half a system. Shine a measured dose of UV on it, and splash it with a measured dose of a bleach-like chemical. Does it live?
3 The Mars poison, and breathing without air ⬅ the crux 5: pcrA, pcrB, pcrC, Daro_2580, fccA
7,065 bp of genes		 Clearing the poison and breathing without air are the same trick: the cell breathes the poison instead of oxygen. The gene that helps it breathe without air belongs here, not off on its own. Three measurements at once: is the poison gone, is it still making fertiliser, and can it grow with no air?
4 Making food, and building the home 3: cbbM, prkA, tasA
3,060 bp of genes		 Two of these genes make food out of thin air; the third builds the slimy home it gets made in. All of it is about settling in rather than hanging on. Does it grow with no food added at all, and does it build itself a home you can see?
5 Breaking down what is dead 4: Hevamine-A, cel1, estA, AMY1.1
5,133 bp of genes		 Four tools the cell pushes out through its own wall, all chewing up something lying outside it. This is the earthworm’s actual job. Put it on plant fibre, shells, starch and fat. Do they disappear?
6 Unlocking the rock 2: FRE3, phyA
3,522 bp of genes		 Prising iron and phosphorus out of solid stone. Useful on its own, and nothing else depends on it, so if the money runs out, this is the safest place for it to run out. Does iron and phosphorus come out of ground-up Mars-like rock?

Round order matters. The cheap, certain rounds come first so that a failure costs as little as possible, and round 3 is the crux, the one place the whole single-organism design is a bet rather than a procedure. Two rounds of proof come before it.

The five plans

Ranked by our preference, and each worked out end to end. The last two cannot carry the whole build on their own. They are here because they are the obvious things to try, and it is worth showing why they do not work.

1. A long-synthesis vendor

About this plan Detail
In one line Order each cassette as a single long piece of linear DNA from a vendor that synthesises past the usual 5,000 bp limit.
Biggest piece it can deliver 10,000 bp
What you would order 7 rounds, 7 tubes
Can it carry every round? ⚠️ yes, but 1 of the 6 rounds has to be split in two to fit
How it works • One order, one tube, one piece per round
• GenScript’s GenWand service makes linear double-stranded DNA to 10,000 bp, the form this build wants, straight into the cell (cited)
• Everything else is identical to the simplest plan: resuspend, starve the cells, add the DNA
Why it is good • The simplest possible protocol. Nothing changes about the lab work at all
• One piece means no co-transformation penalty, which is the one real cost of the overlapping-fragment route
• Linear, so no plasmid, no E. coli, no digest. Unlike the clonal route, the extra length costs no extra handling
The catch • Round 3 misses the 10,000 bp ceiling by about 1%. Trimming the homology arms by 60 bp each, or leaving the reporter off that one round, brings it under. Unfixed, this plan splits into 7 rounds instead of 6
• Long synthesis is slower and more expensive per base
• Sequences past 5,000 bp are quoted case by case, so the price is not knowable from a catalogue
Done in this microbe before? The product exists and is sold: GenWand, linear dsDNA to 10 kb. Nothing host-specific to prove.
Round it has to split • The Mars poison, and breathing without air → Detoxify the Mars poison (8,366 bp) + Live without oxygen (4,664 bp)

Everything still gets made. But splitting a round costs an extra round, an extra marker, and leaves a test that no longer covers one whole idea.

2. Overlapping fragments, assembled by the cell

About this plan Detail
In one line Order each cassette as two or three overlapping pieces and let the cell stitch them together on the way in.
Biggest piece it can deliver no limit on the cassette, since it is delivered as several pieces of up to 5,000 bp
What you would order 6 rounds, 13 tubes
Can it carry every round? ✅ yes, all of them, exactly as designed
How it works • Each piece is ordered as an ordinary linear gene fragment, under the 5,000 bp synthesis limit
• Consecutive pieces share about 200 bp of identical sequence
• All pieces go into the same starved culture at once
• The same homologous recombination that inserts the cassette also joins the pieces, with no enzyme, no cloning, no extra step
Why it is good • No new tool and no new organism. It uses the exact mechanism the design already depends on
• No ceiling. A cassette of any size is just more pieces
• Every piece is an ordinary catalogue order from any vendor
• Still no E. coli, no plasmid, no restriction digest
The catch • Efficiency falls with every extra piece. One cell has to take up all of them. Three pieces is much rarer than one
• Needs more DNA and more cells to compensate, which means ordering more material (asserted: standard practice in naturally competent bacteria, but not sourced for this host)
Done in this microbe before? The mechanism is native to the host. The multi-fragment version is standard in other naturally competent bacteria.

Every round fits, in its natural group. Nothing is forced apart.

The three routes we did not pick, worked out anyway

3. CRISPR-associated transposase (CAST)

About this plan Detail
In one line Use a Cas protein fused to a transposon to insert the cassette, instead of relying on the cell’s own recombination.
Biggest piece it can deliver 10,000 bp
What you would order 7 rounds, 7 tubes
Can it carry every round? ⚠️ yes, but 1 of the 6 rounds has to be split in two to fit
How it works • The cassette is carried on a delivery plasmid alongside the CAST machinery
• The guide RNA, not homology arms, chooses the landing site
• Several cassettes can be inserted in one round
Why it is good • Payloads to about 10,000 bp, enough for 5 of the 6 rounds whole
• Multiplexing could collapse the build further still
• Does not depend on the host’s own recombination being efficient
The catch • Nobody has demonstrated CAST in Azotobacter vinelandii (asserted). Making it work would be a project in itself
• Brings back a delivery plasmid, and with it E. coli
• The host does not need it. It already takes up DNA and recombines it, so this replaces a working native mechanism with an unproven imported one
Done in this microbe before? Demonstrated in E. coli and some Gram-negatives. Not in this host.
Round it has to split • The Mars poison, and breathing without air → Detoxify the Mars poison (8,366 bp) + Live without oxygen (4,664 bp)

Everything still gets made. But splitting a round costs an extra round, an extra marker, and leaves a test that no longer covers one whole idea.

4. Clonal gene in a plasmid

About this plan Detail
In one line Order each cassette pre-cloned into a vector, amplify it in E. coli, cut it out and transform.
Biggest piece it can deliver 5,000 bp
What you would order 12 rounds, 6 tubes
Can it carry every round? ❌ no. 6 of the 6 rounds have to be broken up, and 6 of the pieces are still too big to order
How it works • Arrives as a finished circular plasmid
• Amplified in lab E. coli, mini-prepped, linearised with a restriction enzyme
• Then the same starvation and plating as every other route
Why it is good • Sequence-verified. The vendor clones and sequences it, so a failure is never a typo
• The plasmid is a renewable stock, so retries are unlimited
The catch • The ceiling barely moves: 5 kb, perhaps 7 kb (cited), so most rounds still do not fit. The table above costs the cautious 5 kb; at 7 kb only one piece would be blocked instead of six, so this plan is worth re-checking with the vendor before it is ruled out
• Adds back two days, an organism, a miniprep and a digest, which is everything the direct route removed
• Costs more per construct
Done in this microbe before? Entirely routine. The problem is the ceiling, not the method.
Pieces it cannot make Survive drying out, General damage control, Detoxify the Mars poison, Make food out of thin air, Break down dead plants, Unlock minerals from rock

This plan cannot deliver 6 of its rounds. Those genes would need a second method as well, so it is never a plan on its own.

5. One linear fragment, straight into the cell

About this plan Detail
In one line The simplest thing that can work: one ordered piece, resuspend, transform.
Biggest piece it can deliver 5,000 bp
What you would order 12 rounds, 6 tubes
Can it carry every round? ❌ no. 6 of the 6 rounds have to be broken up, and 6 of the pieces are still too big to order
How it works • Order the cassette as a single linear gene fragment
• Resuspend and add it to the starved culture. That is the whole protocol
Why it is good • The fewest steps of any route. Five lab days, one organism
• Cheapest per round
• This is the method most of the other plans build on. Plans 1, 2 and 4 are all ways of getting a bigger cassette to this same starting point
The catch • The 5,000 bp ceiling breaks the grouping. Biological rounds have to be split back into single traits, and some still do not fit
• No sequence verification on a plain fragment
Done in this microbe before? The mechanism is native to the host, and this is the single-piece case of it.
Pieces it cannot make Survive drying out, General damage control, Detoxify the Mars poison, Make food out of thin air, Break down dead plants, Unlock minerals from rock

What we would say to the lab

Start with round 1, whichever plan you pick. It is the cheapest round and the most certain, and it proves the switch works in this host before anything larger is paid for. It is 6,248 bp, already past the ordinary 5,000 bp catalogue limit, so even the first round needs one of the plans above. There is no version of this build that starts with a plain catalogue fragment.

Our preference is a long-synthesis vendor. GenScript’s GenWand builds to 10,000 bp as one linear piece, which carries 5 of the 6 rounds in a single tube and changes nothing about the lab work. Round 3 is 110 bp over that ceiling, and the design page sets out the two ways round it. Which one to take is your call, not ours.

Overlapping fragments is the fallback, and a good one. It has no ceiling at all, and it uses the exact mechanism the design already depends on. A lab that already runs CAST should use what it knows instead.

The biology does not change between these plans. Only the delivery does.
