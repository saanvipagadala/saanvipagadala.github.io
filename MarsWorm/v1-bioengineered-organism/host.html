The host question

MarsWorm’s 37 genes have to live inside a real bacterium. The design picked Azotobacter vinelandii. Two purple bacteria look like better choices, and Rhodopseudomonas palustris looks like the best of the three.

Photosynthesis is close to a moot point. The microbe has to work down in the dirt, not on the surface, and less than 1% of sunlight gets past 2 millimetres of soil.

So the whole thing turns on one question. In the dark, could R. palustris breathe the perchlorate that Mars has everywhere, and fix nitrogen that way?

If yes, it is possibly the better host.

Surprisingly, R. palustris turns up already doing 4 of them, including the two carbon jobs that the current design with A. vinelandii has to borrow from purple bacteria anyway.

The remaining 19 have to be added by gene editing. None needs inventing. Something on Earth already does each one, so the design copies its gene.

Printed at the same price per letter, that is about $1,600 to $3,500, against $2,900 to $6,200 today.

Of those 21 genes, the 4 under Detoxify the Mars poison, copied from Dechloromonas aromatica, matter more than the other 17 together. They are in the order to do two jobs, breaking the poison down and finishing it off, so that the dirt stops being poisonous.

If those same 4 genes can also let the cell breathe perchlorate, everything above holds. If they cannot, R. palustris stays a good idea that cannot go underground.
