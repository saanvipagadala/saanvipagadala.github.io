## 🚀 The Challenge

Every few months, [ORISE](https://orise.orau.gov/k12/students/competitions.html) invites
K–12 students across the United States to do something wonderfully difficult: **imagine
yourself in a STEM career, and share your vision of your future in the field.**

Two winners are chosen per grade band. In March 2026, out of entries from schools across
the country, **Saanvi took one of the two Grades 3–5 prizes.**

## 🔴 The Submission

Most entries answer the question. This one *time-travels.*

Saanvi chose **Martian Colonization Bioengineering** — the science of engineering living
things, plants, animals, and the human body itself, to survive and thrive on Mars, and
designing the habitats that make it all possible. How do we grow food, protect human
health, and build a living ecosystem on another planet?

Then, instead of describing that career, she **wrote the newspaper article she hopes
someone will write about her one day.**

### 📰 *The Mars Gazette* — Friday, July 4, 2055

> **Dr. Saanvi Makes Mars Bloom**
> *How a Girl from California Who Once Cried Over a Tomato Seed Became the Scientist Who
> Brought Life to Another Planet*

A full front page from thirty years in the future: the drop caps, the three columns, the
pull quotes, the interview, the fact box — reporting on the day Dr. Saanvi, Chief Martian
Colonization Bioengineer at Mars Base Alpha, made history.

## 🌱 The Science Is Real

The future is invented. **The science underneath it is not.** Every claim in the article
grows out of published NASA research, cited at the foot of the page:

| The Gazette imagines | NASA actually did |
|---|---|
| 🥬 14 species of Mars crops | Lettuce, tomatoes, radishes and chile peppers grown aboard the ISS |
| 🐝 Honeybees and monarchs in the domes | Decades of proving animals can survive in space |
| 🏠 Seven interconnected bio-domes | CHAPEA — volunteers living a full year in a 3D-printed Mars habitat |
| 🦴 Bone-density countermeasures | 1–1.5% bone loss per month documented in low gravity |
| ☢️ Polymer radiation shielding | Mars has no magnetic field; the surface is bombarded by solar radiation |

## ✨ The Spark

The story begins with a seven-year-old planting a tomato seed in a paper cup on her
bedroom windowsill, and crying when it sprouted, *"Because I made something alive."*

It was inspired by a Christmas-break documentary about **Demis Hassabis** — not for his
Nobel Prize, but for how the film travelled back to his childhood and forward into what
his work might mean. *That* is how I want to tell my story, Saanvi thought. And then she
did.

Her role model, quoted in the article, is **Sally Ride**:

> *"You cannot be what you cannot see."*
> *"When I saw Sally Ride in space, I saw myself on Mars."*
